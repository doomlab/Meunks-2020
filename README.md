# CREP Replication of Muenks et al. (2020), Study 1

A Collaborative Replications and Education Project (CREP) replication of Study 1 from:

> Muenks, K., Canning, E. A., LaCosse, J., Green, D. J., Zirkel, S., Garcia, J. A., & Murphy, M. C. (2020). Does my professor think my ability can change? Students' perceptions of their STEM professors' mindset beliefs predict their psychological vulnerability, engagement, and performance in class. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*.

Conducted at Missouri State University.

## Overview

Participants watch a video clip of a professor going over course expectations, in which the professor expresses a fixed mindset, a growth mindset, or a control message. They then rate their anticipated belonging, evaluative concerns, course engagement, course interest, anticipated course performance, and dropout intentions.

**Differences from the original study**

- A second course condition: participants are told the clip is from the first day of either a Calculus I course or an Introduction to Astronomy course. This tests whether results hold in a STEM course where math is not salient.
- The sample is college students enrolled in at least one psychology course on our campus, rather than an online research platform.

**Hypothesis:** relative to the control and growth mindset conditions, students in the fixed mindset condition will report more evaluative concerns, less anticipated engagement, less interest, and lower anticipated performance.

**Planned analyses** (following the original study): correlations among variables, ANCOVAs with race, gender, and personal mindset beliefs as covariates, and mediation analyses with professor mindset as the predictor, psychological vulnerability as the mediator, and engagement, interest, anticipated performance, and dropout intentions as outcomes (one model per outcome).
